# Avoid AI Writing Tropes

Use the catalog to improve writing, not to identify who or what wrote it. No phrase proves authorship. Judge patterns in context, and keep the writer's voice ahead of any mechanical rule.

## Revise or draft prose

1. Establish the audience, genre, purpose, desired tone, and any supplied voice sample.
2. Read [references/tropes.md](references/tropes.md). Apply only entries relevant to the draft and genre.
3. Identify clusters of mannerisms, not isolated words. Leave a flagged expression alone when it is the clearest or most natural choice.
4. Preserve facts, qualifications, citations, technical terms, and intentional rhythm. Do not invent personal anecdotes, opinions, quotations, or certainty to make prose sound human.
5. Replace generic framing with concrete claims, evidence, actions, or examples. Vary structure only when it improves the writing.
6. Return the revised prose. Add a short change note only when the user asks for commentary or when a substantive ambiguity needs attention.

## Review prose without rewriting

Distinguish among:

- likely distractions: repeated or interacting mannerisms that noticeably weaken the text;
- context-dependent choices: patterns that may be appropriate for the genre;
- personal preferences: edits that are not supported by a general writing principle.

Quote only the smallest useful excerpts. Explain the effect on the reader and propose a concrete alternative. Never assign an AI-authorship probability from style alone.

## Maintain the catalog

When asked to research or update the catalog:

1. Search current sources and prefer primary research, corpus analyses, style guidance with examples, and transparent methodology.
2. Record the publication date, URL, source type, and what the source actually supports.
3. Add or strengthen an entry only when the evidence supports a recognizable pattern. Separate empirical findings from community observations and house-style preferences.
4. Merge duplicates and preserve useful counterexamples. Avoid turning ordinary words into forbidden vocabulary.
5. Mark disputed, weak, or newly observed patterns as `tentative`; mark well-supported patterns as `supported`; mark subjective choices as `preference`.
6. Update the `Last researched` date only after completing a real source review.
7. Keep SKILL.md concise. Put catalog detail and citations only in [references/tropes.md](references/tropes.md).

Make conservative edits. If no new evidence clears the bar, leave the catalog unchanged and report that result.
